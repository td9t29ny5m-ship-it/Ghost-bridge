You don't procrastinate because you're lazy. You do it to feel better, right now.

Psychologists Fuschia Sirois and Tim Pychyl call it a mood problem, not a time problem. The task feels bad, so you dodge the feeling. Instant relief.

But the dread doesn't leave. It piles up.

And here's the twist. In a Stanford brain scan, thinking about your future self looked like thinking about a stranger. So you hand the job to someone you don't know.

The fix sounds soft, but it works. Students who forgave themselves for putting off one exam procrastinated less on the next.

So forgive yourself. Make the first step tiny. And just start.
